Day 7 · Manufacturing economics and scale

A retail BOM is not a factory cost.

Unit economics must include every resource consumed to deliver a conforming product: purchased parts, converted material, labour, machine time, scrap, inspection, packaging, freight, warranty, support and cash tied up while inventory moves.

RFQ template — send the same controlled package to every supplier

Subject: RFQ — [part number / revision] — quantities 100 / 1,000 / 5,000

Package: controlled drawing/PDF, neutral CAD where required, material/process specification, inspection plan, packaging requirement, forecast and confidentiality terms. State which document wins if there is a conflict.

  1. Quote unit price separately for 100, 1,000 and 5,000 conforming units.
  2. State minimum order quantity (MOQ), minimum batch, sample cost and tooling/non-recurring engineering charges.
  3. State sample, first-article and production lead times plus monthly capacity and capacity reserved for us.
  4. Identify exact material grade/brand, process, manufacturing location and all subcontracted operations.
  5. Confirm drawing revision and how revisions are controlled.
  6. Describe prior-notification process for component, material, tooling, process or manufacturing-site changes.
  7. Provide product-change notification (PCN) and end-of-life (EOL) notice periods.
  8. List inspection equipment, sampling, certificates, first-article report and lot traceability supplied.
  9. State warranty, defect containment, return-material and corrective-action process.
  10. Propose qualified alternates, clearly identifying any difference requiring validation.
  11. State Incoterm, origin, packaging, shipping dimensions/weight and export documentation.
  12. State payment terms, quote currency, validity, deposit, cancellation and liability for excess inventory.

Required response: do not accept “same quality” or “equivalent” without identified material/process and evidence. Supplier quotes are commercial evidence only after the specification and terms match.

Measurements required before BOM freeze

  • As-built mass and centre of gravity with exact battery, straps and props.
  • Propeller/frame/wire clearance under assembly tolerance and blade flex.
  • Static propulsion current/thrust on the exact chosen prop, plus motor/ESC temperature.
  • Hover and representative-flight current, usable flight time with reserve and battery sag.
  • FC vibration logs, handling, failsafe and video/control behaviour.
  • Crash/repair record, fastener retention and actual assembly/rework time.
  • Georgian GCAA operating requirements and written ComCom RF interpretation.
  • 100/1,000/5,000-unit quotes, revision/PCN/EOL terms and alternates for critical parts.

Exact switch conditions

Switch to 5-inch P1

Only if the sub-300 g/compact target is relaxed, safe test space is secured, a complete candidate remains inside the authorised budget, and its propulsion/electronics receive materially better verified supply, fit or repair evidence than the four-inch candidate.

Switch to X500-style P1

Only if the purpose changes from cheap subsystem learning to validating a real customer need for stable payload, GNSS/autonomy or modular sensors—and the user explicitly accepts the much higher aircraft, ground, test-space and safety budget.

Stop all aircraft procurement

If Georgian flight/RF conditions cannot be satisfied for the intended test, safe facilities are unavailable, shipping a suitable LiPo is blocked, or the knowledge gates reveal an unresolved interface.

Glossary

Words you should be able to explain.

Aircraft / UAS / drone

The flying vehicle; an unmanned aircraft system (UAS) includes the aircraft and equipment needed to operate it.

FPV

First-person view: live onboard video used by the pilot.

VLOS

Visual line of sight: the remote pilot or allowed observer maintains direct visual awareness as rules require.

FC

Flight controller: computer that estimates motion and commands motors.

ESC

Electronic speed controller: switches battery power into motor phases.

VTX / RX / TX

Video transmitter; receiver; transmitter. Context matters because “TX/RX” also label serial data pins.

LiPo / S / mAh / Wh

Lithium-polymer battery; series-cell count; milliamp-hours of charge; watt-hours of energy.

KV

Approximate motor no-load speed constant in rpm per volt.

BEC / regulator

Circuit that converts battery voltage to a lower controlled voltage such as 5 V.

CoG / MTOM

Centre of gravity; maximum take-off mass including payload.

GNSS / GPS

Global navigation satellite system; GPS is one satellite constellation/system.

Telemetry / OSD

Measurements sent over a link; on-screen display overlays data on video.

CAD / CAM

Computer-aided design; computer-aided manufacturing/toolpath preparation.

DFM / DFA

Design for manufacture; design for assembly.

Datum / tolerance

Declared measurement reference; allowed variation from nominal.

BOM / revision

Bill of materials; controlled version of a design/document.

RFQ / MOQ

Request for quotation; minimum order quantity.

PCN / EOL

Product change notification; end of life.

First article

First part from a new design/revision/process inspected against all requirements before a batch proceeds.

Non-conformance

A requirement was not met; affected material is controlled until an authorised disposition.

Yield

Fraction of attempted units that pass, often measured as first-pass and final yield.

CNC / DXF / STEP / STL

Computer numerical control; common 2D profile exchange; neutral 3D exchange; triangulated print mesh.

CFRP

Carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer.

TPU

Flexible thermoplastic polyurethane used for protective printed parts.

Factory / landed / selling cost

Cost to produce; cost after inbound logistics/duties; customer price after commercial costs and margin.

CAPEX / working capital

Long-lived equipment/facility spending; cash tied up in operating the inventory/receivables cycle.

Day 5 · Assembly, commissioning and safe test

Build evidence in the same order you build the aircraft.

Every step should reduce uncertainty without exposing the aircraft, operator or bystanders to the next hazard too early. Mechanical fit comes before soldering; electrical checks come before power; props-off control checks come before a restrained first hover.

Reliable soldered wiring

Wire gauge

Choose conductor cross-section for expected current, length, allowable voltage drop, flexibility and connector capacity. Signal wire and battery wire solve different problems.

Strain relief

The solder joint should carry electricity, not repeated cable bending or impact loads. Support the cable and leave a service loop without allowing prop contact.

Insulation

Heat-shrink, spacing and abrasion protection prevent conductive contact. Carbon fibre is electrically conductive; do not let bare pads or wires rub the frame.

Connectors

Match type, current, polarity and mating cycles. A hidden adapter is still a required part and a potential failure point.

Fasteners

Correct diameter, pitch, length, head and material matter. Motor screws that are too long can contact windings. Threadlocker must be compatible with nearby plastic.

Routing

Separate sensitive antennas/sensors from high-current switching paths, retain access for repair and keep every cable outside propeller discs.

Fault isolation: change one variable

Observe
what exactly failed?
→
Localise
power, signal, configuration or mechanics?
→
Swap / measure one item
→
Does fault follow the item?
→
Record root cause + corrective action

Example: if one motor stutters, first remove props and inspect it. Swapping the motor with a known position can reveal whether the fault follows the motor or remains with the ESC channel/wiring. Do not replace three components at once and call the result understood.

LiPo handling lifecycle

Before charge

Inspect for swelling, cuts, crushed corners, damaged leads and connector contamination. Confirm cell count, chemistry, charge current and balance connection.

During charge

Attend the process on a noncombustible surface in a planned location. Stop on heat, swelling, odour, cell imbalance or charger error.

Storage

Use the battery/charger manufacturer’s storage-voltage procedure, protect terminals and isolate from heat and combustible material. Log packs by identifier.

After crash

Disconnect only if safe, move people away, observe in a suitable isolated area and do not recharge a pack with suspected damage.

Transport

Protect terminals from short circuit and check carrier rules. FAA guidance requires spare lithium batteries in carry-on baggage and typically limits ordinary passenger batteries by watt-hours; airline rules can be stricter.

Retirement

Quarantine and use a locally approved battery-recycling/disposal route. Do not place a damaged energy pack in ordinary storage or reuse it to “see if it is fine.”

Day 6 · Frame engineering and manufacturing

The frame is a load path, an interface map and a production process.

A frame is not successful because the CAD looks like a drone. It must carry thrust and crash loads, protect interfaces, control vibration, assemble predictably, permit repair and be inspectable with affordable processes.

Follow the load

Primary load path in hover and landing

→

Battery + payload + inertia

→

Hover is not the worst case for a crashable FPV frame. Impacts create short, high loads and can reveal stress concentrations around holes, sharp internal corners and abrupt section changes.

Carbon laminate is directional

Layup

Fibres carry load most efficiently along their direction. A quasi-isotropic plate uses multiple directions—commonly 0°, 90° and ±45°—to reduce directional weakness. “Carbon-looking” surface weave does not disclose the entire internal layup.

Specify supplier, product/grade, thickness tolerance, layup/orientation, resin system or service temperature where relevant, surface finish and lot traceability.

Geometry

Thickness strongly affects bending stiffness, but more thickness adds mass and does not remove stress concentrations. Use generous internal radii, adequate edge distance around holes and supported fastener interfaces.

Replaceable arms localise common crash damage. Sacrificial printed bumpers or mounts should fail harmlessly before expensive electronics or body plates.

Datums and tolerances: beginner example

A datum is a declared reference feature from which other geometry is located. For a flat bottom plate, the primary datum might be one broad face; a mounting-hole axis and an edge can establish the remaining directions. This prevents each inspector from measuring from a different convenient edge.

Do not write ±0.1 mm everywhere. Tight tolerances cost money and may still fail to protect the real function. Start from the interface: motor-hole position must fit the motor; paired plates must align; an arm slot must retain without crushing. Assign tolerances based on function and supplier capability, then verify them with first articles.

Design-for-X checklist

Manufacture

Use available stock thicknesses, cutter access, internal radii, repeatable workholding, nesting and standard tools. Avoid features that require extra setups without functional value.

Assembly

Reduce fastener varieties, prevent reversed parts, give tools access and add harness retention. Track build time and rework.

Inspection

Make critical dimensions reachable with the chosen gauge. Define datums and go/no-go fixtures for repeated interfaces.

Repair

Replace common crash parts without desoldering the entire aircraft. Protect connectors and retain configuration/as-built data.

Packaging

Prevent sharp carbon edges and loose hardware from damaging parts. Include part identity, revision, instructions and count verification.

Supply

Use standard thicknesses, fasteners and interfaces; qualify alternate materials/processes by test rather than assuming equivalence.

Why outsource carbon first

Outsourced cutting teaches the product

You still own requirements, CAD, drawings, material specification, inspection, assembly, tests, revisions and supplier management. You avoid buying a hazardous process before knowing part volume or geometry.

Internal cutting teaches the process

It becomes rational when recurring external cost, lead time, confidentiality or iteration speed outweigh a complete internal cell—including operator time, scrap, maintenance, extraction, metrology and compliance.

Suggested in-house gate: do not purchase a carbon router until (1) an original frame revision has passed mechanical/flight tests, (2) at least two external suppliers have quoted it, (3) expected monthly demand and saved lead time are recorded, (4) a separate workspace and extraction design are approved, and (5) the payback model includes labour, scrap, tools, maintenance and downtime.

Day 3 · Propulsion and electrical sizing

Choose a propulsion system, not isolated parts.

Start with mission and estimated take-off mass. That constrains the propeller. The propeller constrains motor size and speed. Motor plus propeller plus battery voltage determines current. The ESC and battery must safely supply that current.

Inside a brushless motor

◎

Stator

Stationary steel laminations and copper windings create a rotating magnetic field. “1404” usually means an approximately 14 mm stator diameter and 4 mm stator height—not the outside motor dimensions.

◉

Rotor / bell

The rotating outer bell carries permanent magnets. The propeller attaches to its shaft or mounting hub.

↻

Three phases

The ESC rapidly switches battery power through three motor wires. Swapping phase order or changing ESC settings changes direction; modern digital protocols also carry control/telemetry.

KV is the approximate no-load speed constant in revolutions per minute per volt. It is not power, quality or torque. Higher voltage and higher KV both push speed upward; larger props require more torque and often lower KV. Use the manufacturer’s motor-prop-voltage test table, not KV alone.

The ESC is the power valve

An electronic speed controller (ESC) turns flight-controller commands into timed current through a brushless motor’s three phases. A four-in-one ESC contains four channels. Its continuous rating must exceed measured or manufacturer-tested current for the chosen motor-prop-voltage combination with thermal margin. A “burst” rating is time- and cooling-dependent; it cannot rescue an undersized continuous design.

Electrical checks

  • Battery cell count within ESC and motor limits
  • Expected per-motor current below the real channel limit
  • Total connector, wire and battery current capability
  • Regulated 5 V rails sized for camera, receiver and VTX
  • Capacitor voltage rating and polarity

Control checks

  • Flight-controller output protocol supported by ESC firmware
  • Correct motor order and direction
  • Telemetry compatibility if used
  • Accessible firmware/configuration record
  • Cooling representative of the intended installation

LiPo: voltage, energy and risk

S count

Cells in series add voltage. A conventional LiPo is about 3.7 V nominal per cell: 4S is about 14.8 V nominal. Actual voltage changes with charge and load.

Capacity

Milliamp-hours (mAh) describe charge capacity. Convert to amp-hours by dividing by 1,000. A larger pack stores more energy but also adds mass.

Watt-hours

Energy estimate: nominal volts × amp-hours. Watt-hours compare packs of different voltages better than mAh alone.

C-rating

A claimed discharge multiplier: theoretical current = capacity in Ah × C. Hobby labels are not enough for production qualification; measure sag and temperature.

Voltage sag

Internal resistance makes loaded voltage fall. Excessive sag reduces available thrust and produces heat even if open-circuit voltage looked healthy.

Balance lead

Allows a balance charger to observe individual cell voltages. The main lead carries propulsion current; connector type and polarity must match the aircraft and charger.

LiPo rule: charge only with a compatible balance charger and verified settings, attend charging, use a noncombustible location, protect against short circuit and retire damaged or swollen packs. Lithium batteries can enter thermal runaway after damage, overheating or overcharge. FAA lithium battery hazard/transport overview [S14]

Control link and FPV link are different

ExpressLRS control

A radio-control system using a transmitter and compatible receiver. Binding configuration, frequency band, packet rate, antenna installation, serial protocol and failsafe behaviour must match. Link quality is telemetry about the control link, not a guarantee of safe range.

ExpressLRS firmware options [S9]

FPV video

Analog FPV sends an analog television signal over a radio carrier; digital systems encode/compress video and require matching digital hardware. Video quality can fail while control still works—or vice versa—so loss responses must be planned separately.

Why the laptop does not directly receive analog 5.8 GHz FPV: ordinary laptop Wi-Fi hardware is not a tunable analog FPV television receiver. A laptop needs a compatible 5.8 GHz receiver and usually a video-capture interface; this can add latency. A direct analog monitor contains the required receiver/display path.

Day 1 · Categories and applications

Start with the job, not the propeller.

A “drone” is not one product category. Architecture follows mission: hover and close inspection favour multirotors; long-area coverage favours fixed wings; carrying cameras, flying FPV and mapping the ground impose different requirements.

✣

Multirotor

Several motor-propeller units create lift and steer by changing relative thrust.

  • Hovers and takes off vertically
  • Mechanically simple
  • Good close-access platform
  • Consumes power continuously to stay airborne
  • Shorter endurance than an efficient wing
  • Cannot glide safely like a fixed wing
➤

Fixed wing

A wing creates lift while the aircraft moves forward; a propeller mainly overcomes drag.

  • Efficient over distance
  • Longer coverage per battery
  • Suitable for corridor and area mapping
  • Cannot hover
  • Needs launch/landing space or mechanisms
  • Less useful for close stationary inspection
↥

VTOL

Vertical take-off and landing combines hover hardware with wing-borne cruise.

  • Vertical launch plus efficient cruise
  • Useful where runways are unavailable
  • More actuators, software and failure modes
  • Higher mass, cost and integration burden
  • Poor first beginner aircraft

Day 2 · Flight physics, size and geometry

Four forces. Three rotations. One unstable machine.

A quadcopter stays up when total upward thrust equals its weight. It moves by tilting the thrust vector; it rotates by deliberately making motor thrust unequal. The flight controller repeats these corrections many times per second.

Frame names describe different measurements

4″ PROP

4-inch FPV

“Four inch” usually means nominal propeller diameter, not motor-to-motor wheelbase. Packaging is compact and clearances are tight.

5″ PROP

5-inch FPV

Five-inch propellers and a larger footprint provide more packaging room and propulsion choices, but increase kinetic energy and test-space needs.

500 mm

X500-style

“500” refers to an approximately 500 mm diagonal motor wheelbase. Typical frames use larger props, tube arms, landing gear and payload decks.

True-X, dead-cat and clearance

True-X

Motors form a symmetric X. Roll and pitch leverage are similar, which makes dynamics and structural repetition simple. Forward propellers may enter a wide camera view.

Dead-cat

Front arms spread or shift so forward propellers stay out of the camera image. Geometry becomes less symmetric and the mixer/tune must match the real motor positions.

Propeller clearance is a hard interface

At minimum, the rotating discs must not intersect each other, the frame, battery, wires or camera. Real clearance must also allow blade flex, manufacturing tolerance and crash deformation.

tip gap = centre distance − prop diameter

For adjacent equal propellers. A positive geometric gap is necessary, but the acceptable operating margin must be proven for the chosen propeller and frame.

Why centre of gravity matters

The centre of gravity (CoG) is the balance point of the complete aircraft, including battery and payload. If it is displaced, the controller must maintain unequal motor thrust even in level hover. That consumes control margin and can heat one side. PX4 recommends placing the flight controller close to the CoG and configuring its offset when that is not possible. PX4 mounting guidance [S4]

The 250 g line

Georgia’s current GCAA portal describes an Open category for aircraft below 25 kg under specified operational conditions, and a C0 class below 250 g with additional design limits. Privately built and class-labelled aircraft are not automatically the same legal object. Treat 250 g as an important design target, not a statement that this prototype qualifies for a particular class or operation. Always check registration, subcategory, training, airspace and operating conditions before flight. GCAA UAS portal [S1]

Measurement rule: “designed to be under 250 g” is not “under 250 g.” Compliance uses the finished maximum take-off mass, including battery and payload, measured on a suitable scale.
