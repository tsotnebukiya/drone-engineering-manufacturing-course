### Bench layout

Keep one clean half for inspection and electronics and one working half for soldering. Put the iron stand on the dominant-hand side, extractor behind the joint rather than behind your face, and the power strip where one action can de-energize the bench. The fire exit path must remain clear.

Do not solder directly on the melamine desk. Use the silicone mat and place a sacrificial ceramic tile or metal tray under any later battery-charging area. Batteries should not be charged on the soldering bench while other work is happening.

### Choosing the iron

The **T210 is the recommended first station**, not because maximum temperature matters, but because a cartridge heater responds quickly and the compact format is useful long term. Tip geometry matters more than chasing a temperature number: use a small chisel for ordinary pads and a broad chisel/hoof for battery leads or ground planes. A needle tip is usually the wrong default because it transfers heat poorly.

The T12 is the value choice. The TS101 is only the portability choice; once a suitable power supply, cable, stand, and tip are included, it is not the cheapest local setup. Do not buy the combined hot-air station for this course—the heat gun below is only for heat shrink, and board-level hot-air rework can wait.

Practice in this order:

1. Tin and clean the tip; make ten separate bright, concave through-hole joints.
2. Solder a header straight without melting its plastic or bridging adjacent pads.
3. Strip fine silicone wire without nicking strands; tin it without solder wicking far under the insulation.
4. Attach and remove fine wires from isolated pads. Inspect under magnification and test continuity.
5. Intentionally make and then repair a bridge with flux and wick.
6. Use paired perfboard pads to simulate larger ESC pads; attach 18 AWG wire with the broad tip.
7. Pull-test only after the joint cools. Photograph one acceptable and one rejected joint and record iron temperature/tip used.

Advance to real electronics only when joints are repeatable, insulation is not scorched, no strands are loose, adjacent pads do not bridge, and the meter result matches the circuit you intended.

## Safety rules that are part of the purchase plan

1. Wear eye protection for soldering, clipping wire, heat-shrink work, and every first power-up.
2. Capture fumes at the joint and ventilate the room. A small carbon-filter fan is source capture, not a substitute for fresh-air exchange.
3. With leaded solder, keep food and drink off the bench, clean the surface, and wash hands before touching your face or leaving the work area. Lead exposure can occur through contaminated hands and surfaces, not only visible smoke.
4. Put the iron only in its stand. Unplug it when leaving. Keep IPA closed and far from hot tools.
5. Before power-up: **props physically removed**, polarity checked at every connector, continuity/short check completed, wiring inspected under magnification, and smoke stopper fitted.
6. Stop immediately if the current limiter trips, anything heats unexpectedly, or smell/smoke appears. Disconnect at the source; do not keep retrying.
7. Charge only the confirmed chemistry and cell count, with the correct balance connection and charger profile. Remain present and awake. Never charge a swollen, punctured, crashed, wet, or hot pack.
8. The ABCE extinguisher helps with ordinary/electrical or surrounding bench fire. It is not proof that LiPo thermal runaway will stop. Establish the battery maker's isolation/cooling/emergency procedure before batteries enter the room.
9. Keep damaged or suspect batteries out of normal household waste and obtain local hazardous-battery disposal guidance; do not improvise puncturing or salt-water disposal.

## Checkout checklist

Before paying:

- [ ] Seller has physically confirmed the exact model and price today.
- [ ] T210/T12 package includes a safe stand and the intended handpiece.
- [ ] At least one useful small chisel and one broad pad/battery-wire tip are available for that exact station.
- [ ] Meter probes, batteries, manual, and continuity buzzer work before leaving the shop.
- [ ] Extractor runs and replacement filters are available.
- [ ] Mat dimensions are confirmed in centimetres, not the listing's apparent typo.
- [ ] Wire stripper is tested/confirmed for fine stranded silicone wire.
- [ ] Safety glasses carry the stated standard markings.
- [ ] Extinguisher has an intact seal, readable pressure/service status, and future inspection/refill route.
- [ ] All chemical containers are sealed, labeled, and within usable date.
- [ ] No acid flux, permanent threadlocker, charge-only USB cable, or incompatible charger has slipped into the basket.

Before leaving Stage 1:

- [ ] At least 20 repeatable acceptable joints have been made on sacrificial board.
- [ ] A solder bridge has been created, found under magnification/meter, and repaired.
- [ ] Fine wire and 18 AWG wire can be stripped, tinned, attached, and pull-checked without damage.
- [ ] The user can explain what continuity mode proves—and what it cannot prove.
- [ ] The bench can be de-energized immediately and has no food, drink, clutter, or exposed flammables.

Before first aircraft power:

- [ ] Fresh aircraft and battery decisions are documented.
- [ ] Correct smoke stopper/current limiter is physically present and tested on a known load.
- [ ] Correct charger, leads, balance connection, and noncombustible charging/storage plan are present.
- [ ] Props are physically off the aircraft.
- [ ] Polarity, connector orientation, solder joints, insulation, and continuity have passed inspection.

## Freshness and limits

This is a researched local purchasing plan as of **12 August 2026**, not a guarantee of inventory, seller quality, regulatory compliance, or product authenticity. The most important unresolved local gaps are intentionally visible: handled RC hex drivers, a ready-made FPV smoke stopper, and a defensible LiPo charging/storage solution. Do not erase those gaps by substituting an unknown marketplace product without verifying the exact specification.

The tool path is intentionally close to later manufacturing work: controlled soldering, fixturing, inspection, electrical checks, measurement, safety discipline, and written acceptance criteria all transfer. It does not pretend that retail availability proves supply at 1,000–5,000 units; production tooling and consumables will require supplier qualification, calibration plans, lot traceability, alternates, and RFQs after the prototype process is known.
